Before signing a restaurant lease, know whether the site can work.

01 / PROBLEM DEFINITION

The most expensive mistake happens before the lease is signed.

A restaurant lease is a long, hard-to-reverse commitment made with incomplete information. The gap is not a lack of advice; it is the lack of a verdict that can be recomputed and challenged. For first-time founders — many of them immigrants — the sharper pain is not knowing what they do not know: NNN rent, liquor-license timelines, ADA obligations, personal guarantees.

01 · Observe

Demand signal: restaurant-consulting livestreams in China. Exploratory observation, not a quantified audience study. It produced a hypothesis, not proof.

02 · Frame

Three questions before commitment: can this rent be supported, which costs are missing, and what still has to be checked?

03 · Differentiate

A general LLM can sound confident without reproducing the same numbers twice. The wedge is a verdict whose every input can be opened and recomputed.

04 · Constrain

US-first, English, USD / sqft, Austin as the initial rule pack. Not served: legal or financial advice, ADA / zoning / permit verdicts, children under 13, and v1 ghost kitchens.

Evidence added during case preparation · 27 Sep 2026

The National Restaurant Association 2025 operations release reports median pre-tax income of 2.8% of sales for full-service and 4.0% for limited-service restaurants. SBA guidance and Austin permitting sources add cost and diligence context. These are background signals, not universal thresholds and not evidence of willingness to pay.

02 / SCENE JUDGMENT

The scene dictated the architecture and the business model.

Site search is a 2–4 week burst, often in basements or unfinished units with weak connectivity, and the decision is irreversible. Those are constraints, not background.

Scene factProduct consequenceWhat it ruled out
A 2–4 week burst of site visitsSingle-use credits and a pack, not a recurring planMonthly subscription
Weak or no connectivity on siteCore math and the verdict run fully offline on-deviceCloud-dependent evaluation
Irreversible, high-cost commitmentReproducible verdict plus a frozen report snapshotA score that changes after a rule update

Who it is for

First-time restaurant founders and site-selection consultants evaluating a shortlisted US site; immigrants navigating unfamiliar local rules.

Who it is not for

Landlords, chain expansion teams, anyone seeking legal / zoning / permit conclusions, or a market-wide traffic dataset.

Thresholds (rent-to-sales, prime cost, the 25% feasibility gate) are product rules calibrated from public benchmarks, not predictions. They require local calibration city by city.

03 / THE AI BOUNDARY

Give the model the smallest possible job.

The AI is a bounded qualitative assistant, not the decision maker. Every number, score and feasibility verdict is computed locally by pure Swift code. The model adds optional wording around a verdict it cannot change.

FORBIDDEN

No financial results, no scores or score adjustments, no legal / permit / zoning conclusions, no change to the feasibility gate. A ±15-point "AI adjustment" proposed in an early review was removed from the specification.

ALLOWED

Six optional whitelisted fields of qualitative assistance. All nullable, all labelled "AI inference — not part of the base score", missing fields shown as "—". User-triggered; never on the critical path.

User-triggered branch → optional AI explanation & wording · gpt-4o-mini via a signed Cloudflare Worker · no financial calculation or scoring

GuardImplementationWhat it protects
Output shapeOpenAI Responses API with strict json_schema, 800 max output tokensFree-form model output
Three-layer score banSchema excludes numbers; Worker strips forbidden keys; client parser rejects nine score-like keysNumbers drifting into the model's hands

Trade-off: the AI feels less "smart". In exchange, identical inputs always produce the same verdict, and the assistant can be switched off without breaking the product.

04 / KEY DECISIONS

Four decisions, each with a price.

01

Feasibility before persuasion

A polished negotiation script can keep someone pursuing a weak deal. A feasibility gate computes the rent reduction the assumptions require: ≤10% open, 10–25% caution, >25% blocked. If all three rescue paths fail, the report recommends walking away and withholds the negotiation script.

Rejected alternative: always generate scripts, or present a single score. Verification: GoldenCase pins the three-way failure. Price: 25% is a product rule to calibrate, not a landlord prediction; it can talk a paying user out of a purchase.

02

Show uncertainty where it enters

Diligence uses Yes / No / Unknown. Only "No" reduces a score; "Unknown" reduces confidence and, above five unknowns, locks the score at insufficient instead of inventing a reassuring number. A sensitivity matrix shows five scenarios with their own rent-to-sales and prime cost.

Rejected alternative: treat missing inputs as zero or publish one composite score. Verification: unit tests cover the Unknown paths. Price: some users get no score at all — an incomplete experience that is honest.

03

Local kernel, minimal AI permission

Financial results, scoring, gating and scripts are deterministic local code. The LLM receives only a derived business summary on request and returns six optional qualitative fields that never enter the base score.

Rejected alternative: let the model score, or apply an AI adjustment to the total. Verification: three independent layers reject score keys. Price: the product is less "AI-native" to demo, and several hundred lines of guard code exist to protect a small feature.

04

Verify purchases locally — and overturn a code review

A review recommended server-side confirmation before granting entitlements, and a third-party SDK. I shipped StoreKit 2 JWS verification on-device: grant entitlement first, then sync the ledger in the background, with a persistent retry queue and self-implemented Apple Server Notification v2 signature checks for refunds.

Rejected alternative: gate value on a network round-trip, or add RevenueCat. Verification: 143 Swift tests; a real purchase-to-refund drill is still open. Price: a residual refund-window risk and signature code I own forever.

A decision users can inspect

In this development example, the base scenario shows a monthly loss of $2,762 and rent-to-sales of 20.5% at $42,432 revenue. Pairing the ratio with its revenue assumption makes the result interpretable. It is a worked example, not a forecast for a real restaurant.

05 / SYSTEM DESIGN

Make the product promise an architectural constraint.

01Inputs & assumptions
02Local math & scoring
03Feasibility decision
04Frozen report snapshot

Optional branch → signed Worker → LLM wording → nullable AI fields · never feeds back into the verdict

Reproducible calculations

Swift computes money in integer cents with explicit round-then-propagate rules, so displayed numbers can feed later calculations. A negative month yields "never pays back" rather than infinity or zero.

Stable historical reports

A saved report freezes derived values and its rule version. Current / Previous rule-pack snapshots with force-replace rollback mean a bad pack cannot be archived, and an old report keeps its original conclusion.

Bounded cloud surface

Only the optional AI call and purchase ledger leave the device; the request carries derived metrics, not the address, photos or verdict. HMAC signing, quota ledger and refund handling live in the Worker.

06 / COMMERCIAL & COMPLIANCE

A successful purchase should still feel successful when the ledger fails.

App review exposed a mismatch: a purchase could succeed and grant access while a secondary ledger-sync failure still surfaced as an error. To the customer, the difference between payment and bookkeeping is invisible.

I separated the responsibilities. StoreKit verification anchors local entitlement delivery; the Worker records the transaction afterwards. Ledger failures became silent with a persistent retry queue, and transaction updates reuse the local fulfillment path.

The lesson: failure handling is part of the paid product experience. It must reflect whether the user received value.

07 / VERIFICATION

What has actually been verified?

Engineering validation, product-hypothesis validation and market validation are three different things. Only the first has hard evidence so far.

LayerEvidence availableWhat it does NOT prove
Engineering143 Swift tests / 0 failures; 38 Worker checks / 0 failures; GoldenCase pins the worked example; 2 UI walkthrough tests (~69s, 19 captures); security and IAP reviews; 13-item pre-submission checklistThat anyone wants this, or that the thresholds fit their city
Product hypothesisDeterministic kernel, three-state diligence, frozen snapshots and eight fallbacks are implemented and testableThat founders understand Unknown, accept a blocked verdict, or value the paid AI fields
MarketApp Store release 5 Nov 2026; no download, revenue, rating or retention data yetProduct-market fit. Nothing on this page is a growth result

Worked example pinned by GoldenCase, not a forecast: $42,432 revenue, −$2,762 monthly result, 20.5% rent-to-sales, 2.24 turns to break even, 51.1% reduction required — which triggers the blocked verdict. Engineering counts are engineering validation, not outcomes.

Metrics to collect next

Unknown rate and score-lock frequency; AI-field adoption and regeneration; behaviour after a blocked verdict; $6.99 conversion, refunds and retention; threshold recalibration by city. Each needs a pre-declared bar before a number is called a success.

How I would read them

If users never resolve Unknowns, the diligence UI is too expensive. If paid fields are ignored, remove the tier instead of marketing it harder. If a veto is routinely overridden, the rule — not the user — needs recalibration.
